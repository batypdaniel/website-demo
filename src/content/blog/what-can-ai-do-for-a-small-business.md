It's hard to go a day without hearing about AI. But for a small business owner, the real question is simple: *will this save me time or make me money, and is it safe?* Here's a practical look at where AI is genuinely useful today.

## Answering repetitive questions

AI assistants can answer common questions using your own information - your policies, services, pricing guidelines or employee handbook. Used internally, they help staff find answers quickly. Used on your website, they can handle simple customer questions around the clock and hand off to a person when needed.

## Reading and processing documents

Invoices, intake forms, contracts, purchase orders and emails all contain information someone has to read and re-type. Modern AI models are very good at pulling out the important details and putting them into your systems, with a person reviewing anything unusual.

## Writing first drafts

From job postings and proposals to product descriptions and customer emails, AI can produce a solid first draft in seconds. Your team stays in control of the final version, but spends far less time staring at a blank page.

## Finding patterns in your data

Combined with good data practices, AI and machine learning can help forecast demand, flag unusual transactions or identify customers who might be about to leave.

## What to watch out for

- **Accuracy.** AI can be confidently wrong. Keep a person in the loop for anything important, and design systems that show their sources.
- **Privacy.** Be careful what you paste into free AI tools. Business-grade options with clear data policies are usually worth it.
- **Starting too big.** The best first AI project is small, specific and easy to measure.
