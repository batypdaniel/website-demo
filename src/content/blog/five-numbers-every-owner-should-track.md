When business owners picture "data analytics," they often imagine a wall of charts. In practice, the most useful dashboards are small. Five well-chosen numbers, updated automatically and viewed every week, beat fifty numbers nobody looks at.

## Pick numbers you can act on

A good metric is one where, if it moves, you know what to do about it. "Website visits" is interesting; "quote requests per week" is actionable. For each number you consider, ask: *if this dropped 20% next week, what would I change?*

## A starting point

Every business is different, but most owners get a lot out of some version of these five:

1. **Revenue** - this week vs. the same week last year, so seasonality doesn't fool you.
2. **Gross margin** - by product, service or job type. Growth isn't good news if margin is shrinking.
3. **New customers or leads** - the leading indicator for future revenue.
4. **Repeat or retained customers** - usually the cheapest revenue you'll ever earn.
5. **Cash position and receivables** - how much you have, and how much you're waiting on.

## Automate the updates

A dashboard only works if it's current. The biggest time-saver is connecting it directly to your POS, accounting and CRM systems so it refreshes itself - no more exporting and pasting.

## Then go deeper

Once the basics are in place, it becomes much easier to answer bigger questions: which customers are most profitable, what to stock next month, or where to spend your marketing budget. That's where forecasting and data science come in.
