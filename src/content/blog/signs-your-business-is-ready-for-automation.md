Automation isn't just for big companies with IT departments. Some of the biggest wins we see come from businesses with fewer than 20 people, where a few hours of manual work each week adds up fast. Here are five signs it's time to take a look.

## 1. The same information gets typed in more than once

A new customer fills out a form on your website. Someone copies their details into the CRM. Later, someone else types them into your invoicing system. Every re-type is time lost and a chance for a typo. If you can describe a task as "copy X from here to there," it can almost certainly be automated.

## 2. Your reports take hours to put together

If someone spends the first morning of every month exporting spreadsheets, pasting them together and fixing formulas, that report should be building itself. Automated reports are also more consistent - everyone sees the same numbers, calculated the same way.

## 3. Things fall through the cracks when someone is out

When a process lives in one person's head (or inbox), it breaks as soon as they're on vacation. Automated workflows make sure follow-ups get sent, approvals get routed and deadlines get flagged no matter who's in the office.

## 4. You've outgrown your spreadsheets

Spreadsheets are fantastic - until they're shared by six people, have 40 tabs, and nobody dares touch the formulas. That's usually a sign a process needs a proper tool, or at least an automated pipeline feeding it.

## 5. You're hiring to keep up with admin work

If growth means adding people just to handle data entry and paperwork, it's worth checking how much of that work could be handled automatically first. Automation lets your team focus on customers and the work that actually needs a human.

## Where to start

Start small. Pick one repetitive process that happens at least weekly, write down each step, and estimate how long it takes. That's usually enough to see whether automating it would pay off - and it's exactly what we walk through in a [free consultation](/contact/).
